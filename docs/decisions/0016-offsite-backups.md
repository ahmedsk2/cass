# Off-host backups: age-encrypted copies in a create-only OCI bucket, restored monthly

- **Context.** Every dump and storage archive lived only on the host's disk, `/srv/backups/cass`, so losing or imaging the host lost or exposed them all (`docs/reviews/2026-10-16-expert-brief.md`, B8 and question 4). The spec's NAS sync was never connected.
- **Decision.** The owner's, on 2026-11-06: a private OCI Object Storage bucket in the owner's own tenancy; the host signs in as an instance principal through the OCI CLI, installed for root, with no API key on the host; every file is encrypted with `age` to the owner's public key before it leaves, and the private key stays off the host, in a password manager and on paper; a monthly restore drill from that copy on the owner's PC.
- **The plan's seven defaults, accepted by the owner on 2026-11-14** with the plan's approval (asked "Approve the B8 plan as summarised, with the reviewer's defaults?", answered "Approve the plan"):
  1. The bucket's retention rule is locked after the first good drill.
  2. The bucket stays in the host's region, Riyadh.
  3. Each drill's encrypted download is kept on an external drive until the first drill 51 or more days later: at most about 81 days with a drill a month.
  4. The fence that keeps the containers from the metadata service, and so from the instance principal, is a separate B3 item, not part of this work.
  5. `tools/backup/` joins `CLAUDE.md`'s sensitive paths.
  6. The privacy page says that the encrypted copies away from the server are kept at most 90 days from the day they are sent, which is within a day of each backup.
  7. The drill signs in with a browser session (`oci session authenticate`), never with a stored administrator key.
- **The engineering choices that came with the plan.** `cass-backup-offsite` on its own cron line at 04:17, after both backups, rather than at the end of `cass-backup`, which the release script also runs before a migration, so an OCI failure neither stops a release nor blurs the nightly-dump heartbeat, and each run catches up on what is not yet copied. Only backups younger than 7 days are sent, since a copy lives 90 days from its upload. The script in `tools/backup/`, installed from a green commit of `main` with both ends' SHA-256 compared, so it needs no deploy. A policy letting the host create objects and list them in that one bucket, checked against every other grant before install, hence single-part uploads (a multipart upload needs `OBJECT_READ` and `OBJECT_OVERWRITE`) and names carrying the SHA-256 of the encrypted bytes, so nothing is ever overwritten. A 30-day retention rule instead of versioning (OCI allows one or the other, and the host can neither overwrite nor delete anyway), a lifecycle deleting objects 90 days after upload, a 10 GB quota and an alarm at 8 GB. `age` from Ubuntu's archive on the host, and v1.3.2, checked against GitHub's digest, on the PC. The OCI CLI pinned to 3.96.0, from wheels only, in a root-only virtual environment. The heartbeat pinged from inside the script, so CI tests it, only when every finished backup is off-host and verified and the newest dump is under 30 hours old. The drill restoring the copy the host's manifest names, checked byte for byte against the host's file.
- **Consequence.** The policy bounds what a compromise can do; it does not stop it. Any process on the host, containers included, reaches the metadata service and so the instance principal: it can read the objects' names and sizes and fill the quota with junk that nobody can delete for 30 days, after which real copies fail until the quota is raised. Root on the host can also upload forged copies that decrypt cleanly (it holds the public key; age proves secrecy, not origin), edit the manifest to name them, and keep pinging Kuma while sending nothing (it holds the push URL); after a suspected compromise only copies made before it count, and the drills recorded in `docs/HANDOVER.md` name them. The copies share the host's tenancy and region; the external drive is the copy that survives losing the tenancy. Losing both copies of the private key makes every off-host copy unreadable; each drill proves one copy. Deleted data stays in the bucket at most 90 days from the upload, which follows a backup within a day and never by more than 7 days, and on the external drive at most about 81 days from the drill (about 88 for the archive inside); a month without a drill needs the drive's folders older than 50 days deleted by hand. An erased person's rows survive in backups that long, and a restore brings them back unless the erasure is re-applied. A backup older than 7 days that never went off-host stays on the host only. One recipient only: a second key pair would survive losing the first, at the price of a second secret.
- **To reverse.** Remove the 04:17 cron line, `/usr/local/bin/cass-backup-offsite`, `/opt/oci-cli` and the three root-only files from the host; delete the policies `cass-backup-host-writes` and `cass-backups-lifecycle`, the dynamic group `cass-backup-host`, the alarm `cass-offsite-filling` and the quota `cass-backups-cap`; let the lifecycle empty the bucket, then delete it and the compartment (a locked rule waits for its objects to age out); delete `tools/backup/`, the `Off-host backup script` step in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` and `tools/backup/` in `CLAUDE.md`'s sensitive paths; and take the privacy page's two backup sentences out of `lang/en/public.php` with `tests/Feature/Public/PrivacyPageTest.php`, putting the hosting paragraph's first sentence back to "The service is hosted in Saudi Arabia." in the page and in `tests/Feature/Public/LandingPageTest.php`.
